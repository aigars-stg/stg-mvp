# Brand Voice & Tone Guide

**Second Turn Games**

> *"Every game deserves a second turn"*

---

## Introduction

This guide defines how Second Turn Games communicates across all touchpoints — from marketplace UI and transactional emails to social media and customer support. Our voice reflects the warmth, community spirit, and shared passion of the board game hobby.

Use this guide to ensure every message feels like it comes from the same place: a friendly fellow gamer who happens to run a marketplace.

---

## Our Philosophy

We believe used board games are treasures waiting to be rediscovered, not leftovers. Every game on our platform has stories to tell and adventures yet to come. Our communication celebrates this: we're not selling "secondhand" — we're connecting games with their next players.

### Core Belief

> *Games are meant to be played, not shelved. When a game moves from one collection to another, both the seller and buyer win — and the game fulfills its purpose.*

---

## Voice Pillars

Our voice rests on four pillars. These remain constant across all communication — they're who we are.

### 1. Welcoming

*Like inviting someone to join your game night.*

We're warm, inclusive, and never gatekeep. Whether someone is listing their first game or their hundredth, they belong here. We use "we" and "our community" language that makes everyone feel part of something.

- No jargon barriers or insider-only language
- Celebrate first-time sellers and buyers
- Treat every question as a good question

### 2. Straightforward

*Clear rules make better games.*

We use simple language and get to the point. If something costs money, we say so. If there's a problem, we explain it clearly. No corporate speak, no buried information, no passive voice when active works better.

- One idea per sentence when possible
- Lead with the most important information
- Avoid hedging and unnecessary qualifiers

### 3. Playful

*Games are meant to be fun.*

We have room for wit, game references, and small moments of delight. But we're playful like Mailchimp — we prefer a wink to a shout. Humor supports communication; it never undermines clarity or feels forced.

- Game-related wordplay when it fits naturally
- Celebrate small wins and milestones
- Never joke during problems or payment issues

### 4. Trustworthy

*Fair play matters.*

We're reliable, transparent, and honest about what we are and aren't. When something goes wrong, we own it. Our buyer protection isn't marketing — it's a promise. Trust is earned through consistency, not claims.

- Be upfront about fees, timelines, and processes
- Acknowledge mistakes directly
- Security language in payment contexts

## Tone by Context

*Voice is who we are. Tone is how we speak in different situations.* Our voice stays constant; our tone adapts based on context and the user's emotional state.

### Emotional Calibration Matrix

| User State | Tone Response | Pillars to Emphasize |
|------------|---------------|----------------------|
| Excited | Match their enthusiasm | Playful, Welcoming |
| Confused | Patient, guiding | Straightforward, Trustworthy |
| Frustrated | Calm, solution-focused | Straightforward, Trustworthy |
| Anxious | Reassuring, clear | Trustworthy, Straightforward |
| Neutral | Warm, efficient | All pillars balanced |

## UI Microcopy

Clarity beats cleverness in the interface. Every word should help users accomplish their goals.

### Buttons & Actions

Use action verbs that describe outcomes. Be specific about what happens next.

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| Submit | List your game |
| Process | Complete purchase |
| OK | Got it |
| Cancel | Never mind |

### Empty States

Empty states are opportunities. Be helpful and encouraging, not just descriptive.

**No listings yet**
> That collector's edition gathering dust? Someone's looking for exactly that.

**No messages**
> When buyers have questions about your games, they'll appear here.

**Wishlist empty**
> Save games you're watching. We'll let you know if prices drop.

### Error Messages

Calibrate tone to severity. Light issues get friendly language; serious problems need direct, empathetic responses.

| Severity | Tone | Example |
|----------|------|---------|
| Minor | Light, reassuring | "Oops! Let's try that again." |
| Medium | Helpful, solution-focused | "We couldn't save your changes. Check your connection and try again." |
| Serious | Direct, empathetic | "We're sorry — something went wrong with your payment. Your card wasn't charged. Here's what to do next." |

## Transactional Emails

Get to the point first, then add brand warmth. Subject lines should be clear and specific. Body copy leads with confirmation, then celebrates.

### Email Structure

1. **Subject line:** Clear, specific, no clickbait
2. **Opening:** Confirmation or key information first
3. **Body:** Details and context
4. **Call to action:** One clear next step
5. **Sign-off:** Warm, brief

### Email Examples

#### Order Confirmation

**Subject:** Your order is confirmed — Catan is on its way!

> Nice move! Your purchase of Catan (4th Edition) is confirmed. [Seller name] will ship within 3 business days. We'll send tracking details once it's on the way.

#### Shipping Update

**Subject:** Catan shipped — track your game

> Your game is on the move! Catan shipped today and should arrive by [date]. Track it here: [link]

#### Sale Notification (to Seller)

**Subject:** You sold Catan! Here's what to do next

> Great news — someone bought your Catan listing for €18.00. Ship it within 3 business days and add the tracking number to your dashboard. The full amount will be available in your balance after delivery confirmation.

#### Payment Available

**Subject:** €18.00 is ready to withdraw

> Your sale of Catan is complete, and €18.00 is now in your balance. Request a payout anytime from your Seller Dashboard.

> 1,000 games found new players this month. That's 1,000 game nights made possible. Thanks for being part of this community. 🎲

## Customer Support

Support is where trust is built or broken. Lead with empathy, provide clear solutions, and own our mistakes.

### Support Principles

- **Own the problem:** "Let me help you with this" not "That's handled by..."
- **Lead with action:** What you're doing, then apology if needed
- **Match their energy:** Casual when they're casual, formal when they're upset
- **No blame language:** Focus on solutions, not fault
- **Be human:** It's okay to say "I understand that's frustrating"

### Response Templates

#### Item Not as Described

> I'm sorry the game arrived in different condition than expected. That's not the experience we want for you. I've opened a case with the seller, and you'll hear back within 24 hours. If we can't resolve it, we'll process a full refund. I'll keep you updated.

#### Shipping Delay

> I checked on your order — it looks like the parcel is delayed at the sorting facility. These delays usually resolve within 1-2 days. If it hasn't arrived by [date], reach out and we'll figure out next steps together.

#### Payment Issue

> I can see the payment didn't go through — your card wasn't charged. This usually happens when the card issuer declines the transaction. Try a different payment method, or contact your bank to authorize the purchase. Let me know if you need anything else.

## Words We Use

Specific word choices shape perception. These lists help maintain consistency.

### Preferred vs. Avoided

| ✓ Prefer | ✗ Avoid |
|----------|---------|
| Pre-loved, previously owned | Used, secondhand, old |
| Find, discover | Purchase, acquire, obtain |
| Seller, buyer, player | User, customer, client |
| Community | User base, customers |
| Collection | Inventory, stock |
| Simple, clear, easy | Seamless, frictionless, streamlined |
| Help, assist | Leverage, utilize, facilitate |
| We, our, together | The platform, the company |
| Sorry | We apologize for any inconvenience |
| Let me check, I'll find out | Per our policy, as per the terms |

### Game-Related Terms

Use board game terminology naturally, but explain when context requires it.

- **Punched/unpunched** — whether game tokens have been removed from sheets
- **Complete** — all components present
- **Sleeved** — cards protected in sleeves
- **NIS/Shrink** — new in shrink wrap
- **OOP** — out of print

## Grammar & Style

### Contractions

Use contractions. They're warmer and more natural. "We're" not "We are." "You'll" not "You will." 

*Exception: legal documents and terms of service use formal language.*

### Punctuation

- Use the Oxford comma (games, expansions, and accessories)
- One space after periods
- Avoid exclamation marks in UI copy (save them for celebrations)
- Use em dashes — like this — for emphasis

### Capitalization

- Sentence case for headlines and buttons ("List your game" not "List Your Game")
- Capitalize proper game titles as published
- Don't capitalize common terms (board game, marketplace, collection)

### Numbers & Currencies

- Write out one through nine; use numerals for 10+
- Currency symbol before number: €15.00
- Use periods for decimals (€15.00)

## Baltic Localization

Our voice should feel native in Latvian, Lithuanian, and Estonian — not like translated English.

### Key Differences

| Language | Considerations |
|----------|----------------|
| **Estonian** | Finno-Ugric (not Indo-European). Gender-neutral pronouns. Direct communication style. Strong digital culture — users expect sophisticated UX. Default to formal address (Härra, Proua). |
| **Latvian** | Baltic Indo-European. Gendered nouns and pronouns. Low-context culture — don't over-explain. Formal address with Kungs/Kundze until invited otherwise. |
| **Lithuanian** | Baltic Indo-European (related to Latvian). Direct communication preferred. Formal by default. Get to the point — unnecessary words feel like padding. |

### Localization Principles

- **Transcreate, don't translate** — adapt meaning and emotion, not just words
- **Define intent, not wording** — tell translators what the copy should achieve
- **Use native speakers for review** — machine translation won't capture voice
- **Maintain a terminology glossary** — consistent terms across all content

### Formality Guidance

Default to formal address in all three languages. Marketing and social media can shift informal once brand familiarity is established. UI copy stays moderately formal. Support matches the customer's tone.

## Quick Reference Checklist

Use this checklist before publishing any content.

### Voice Check

- [ ] Does this sound like a friendly fellow gamer?
- [ ] Would I say this at a game night?
- [ ] Is this welcoming to newcomers?
- [ ] Have I avoided jargon or corporate speak?

### Clarity Check

- [ ] Is the most important information first?
- [ ] Can I cut any words without losing meaning?
- [ ] Is there exactly one clear action to take?
- [ ] Would someone understand this in 5 seconds?

### Tone Check

- [ ] Is the tone appropriate for the context?
- [ ] If there's humor, does it support the message?
- [ ] If something went wrong, am I solution-focused?
- [ ] Am I matching the user's likely emotional state?

### Values Check

- [ ] Am I leading with practical value, not preaching?
- [ ] Does this make pre-loved games feel valuable?
- [ ] Am I being honest about fees, timelines, or limitations?
- [ ] Does this build trust or erode it?
